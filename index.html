About Me.

I am a Mechanical Engineering student at The University of Texas at Dallas with a strong focus on robotics, aerospace, and medical device hardware. My engineering journey began in high school through FIRST Tech Challenge Robotics, where I first discovered a passion for research and design.

At UTD, I have applied that R&D mindset across multiple projects, from designing exhaust components on our Formula SAE team to designing and building an L1 high power rocket. These hands on experiences have refined my engineering skills, enabling me to work on industry level projects.

In my free time, I enjoy fishing and playing pickleball with friends. As an Eagle Scout from Troop 219, I stay active outdoors through camping and hiking, and love exploring new travel destinations.

Exhaust Header

I am currently working on creating a full exhaust system for an FSAE internal combustion car. I modeled a multi runner exhaust manifold utilizing SolidWorks to test packaging geometry and practice complex part design.

Future iterations will optimize runner lengths and merge collector geometry to account for engine scavenging and valve overlap timings. I am currently trying to get access to a GT-Suite License for a 1d Engine Simulation to help calculate valve overlap timings using the mass flow from our engine.

FTC Robot Arm

I designed an arm mounted on our robot's vertical extension slides, positioning it to smoothly transfer game pieces directly from our intake to the outtake claw.

The arm features a belt-driven wrist which adds an extra degree of freedom, while the geared claw allows both sides to open and close with the use of a single servo.

The mount for the arm utilizes a 2:3 gear reduction for increased torque and uses parallel plates to take excess force off of the servo.

FTC Custom Chassis

I designed a custom, belt-driven chassis with wheels fully enclosed inside the frame to prevent the robot from snagging on loose game pieces.

Centralizing the motors lowered and balanced the center of gravity, enhancing overall stability when both horizontal and vertical slides are fully extended.

Furthermore, this custom frame allows for precise subsystem placement, greatly reducing the error in our transfer system.

Eagle Scout Lost and Found Cart

I built a custom wooden Lost and Found cart for Renner Middle School. I started out by mapping the whole thing in CAD to get the dimensions, plywood cutout patterns, and screw locations.

In order to make the cart stronger, I used dado and rabbet joints for all the main connections. Making grooves and channels meant I had to learn how to use a table saw and router. The cart is still in active use at the school, allowing staff to roll it outside during dismissal so parents can easily check for missing items.
